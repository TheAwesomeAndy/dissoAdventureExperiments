\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{chap:conclusion}

\section{What This Dissertation Contributes}

This dissertation develops ARSPI-Net as a staged pipeline for transforming trial-averaged EEG into spike-count features, graph-derived summaries, and reservoir-dynamics descriptors. The Stress, Health, and the Psychophysiology of Emotion (SHAPE) Community data provide the empirical application \cite{noauthor_shape_2018}. The central contribution is an inspectable computational workflow: the update equations, temporal bins, fitted linear transforms, graph construction, and summary statistics can each be stated and audited.

The early legacy sweeps record changes in response persistence with membrane decay and an amplitude ordering in one low-threshold realization. Because the original generator for those membrane figures was not retained and its negative values are incompatible with the audited nonnegative-floor update, those plots are qualitative history rather than current-model verification. The corrected noisy early-versus-late pulse experiment is reproducible from the supplied source. Scaling and PCA are fitted inside each training fold; BSC$_6$--PCA-64 gives $99.5\%\pm0.6\%$ held-out-fold accuracy and $99.3\%\pm0.3\%$ across the ten tested reservoir seeds. This supports the feature extractor for that synthetic generator, not optimality or transfer to EEG.

The SHAPE chapters describe several model-derived quantities: per-channel reservoir summaries, within-epoch theta phase-difference concentration, weighted graph metrics, and the coupling norm $\kappa$. Raw SHAPE inputs are not included, so the numerical analyses are not independently reproducible from this package alone. The chapters also document negative results: the tested graph-propagation operators did not improve the archived readout relative to no propagation, many clinical-label comparisons were near chance or failed multiplicity/permutation checks, and concatenating dynamical and topological features did not improve the recorded sample-mean readout performance.

\section{How the Reported Accuracy Results Should Be Read}

The archived SHAPE tables report, among other values, three-condition balanced accuracy of $59.4\%$ for the uncentered reservoir embedding and $78.8\%$ after subject centering; EEGNet and GRU comparators report $89.1\%$ and $78.4\%$, respectively, after the same centering operation. These numbers are retained to document the defended work, but they are not unbiased estimates for a new subject observed under a single condition.

Four post-defense audit findings govern their interpretation. First, the original PCA was fitted globally and, in some scripts, separately by channel. Global fitting allows held-out observations to influence the basis, while separate channel bases make equal-numbered coordinates noncommensurate across nodes. Second, subject centering uses all condition observations for a person, including observations assigned to a nominal test fold. It is therefore a transductive panel normalization, not an inductive preprocessing step for a previously unseen person. Third, the archived primary Chapter~5 extractor divided the full 256-sample epoch into six bins, whereas Chapter~4 specified steps 10--70 and the later attention analysis used that early window. Fourth, the primary script used different random reservoir weights by channel, so its neuron coordinates were not shared across graph nodes. The corrected code applies one saved reservoir to all channels, uses the stated early window, standardizes on training data, and fits one common PCA basis within each fold. It also separates transductive centering from prospective evaluation. Raw SHAPE inputs were not included in the audited source package, so the corrected real-data pipeline could not be rerun. The archived real-data accuracies and PCA/graph results are consequently descriptive legacy estimates for a different preprocessing specification.

The same caution applies to the reported subject and condition sums-of-squares fractions. Values such as 62.6\% subject and 8.7\% condition describe the archived global-PCA representation; they are not fold-independent variance estimates, do not prove that subject and condition occupy identical biological subspaces, and do not show that PCA nuisance removal must fail in other data.

\section{Dynamical, Topological, and Clinical Claims}

The seven reservoir metrics are mathematical summaries of the implemented state trajectory. Their names and equations make them inspectable, but they are not calibrated physiological measurements or biomarkers. The phase-based graph is likewise a graph of within-epoch phase-difference concentration computed from trial-averaged signals; it is not classical across-trial PLV, anatomical connectivity, or evidence of directed neural interaction. Channel labels were unavailable, so anatomical interpretations are not supported.

The coupling norm $\kappa$ summarizes the magnitude of 14 cross-channel rank correlations between dynamical and topological descriptors. In the three-condition analysis, the observed mean exceeds a 5,000-shuffle channel-label null with the corrected Monte Carlo value $p=1/5001\approx0.0002$. This result depends on channel-label exchangeability and does not identify a physiological mechanism. The four-category coupling analysis used a legacy reservoir update and row-level tests with repeated observations; its very small group $p$-value is anti-conservative and is retained only descriptively.

No result in this dissertation validates diagnosis, prognosis, treatment selection, or patient-level explanation. The smallest nominal SUD graph comparison had parametric $p=0.0004$, but its label-permutation value was $p=0.073$. Other clinical results are uncorrected, selected after examining many features, near chance, or inconsistent across unmatched analysis pipelines. They are hypotheses for external study, not disorder signatures.

\section{Engineering Lessons}

Four engineering lessons remain supported after the audit:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Preprocessing is part of the model.} Scaling, PCA, centering, feature selection, and graph construction must be learned from training data within each outer fold. A transformation that uses all observations for a person is transductive and must be labeled as such.
    \item \textbf{Common coordinates matter.} Node features compared or propagated across channels require one shared training-fold basis. Similar accuracy across independently initialized reservoirs does not prove coordinate alignment.
    \item \textbf{Traceability is narrower than explanation.} A BSC$_6$ index identifies a neuron and response interval; a PCA coordinate mixes both. Recording the computational path supports audit and reproducibility, but does not by itself confer physiological or clinical meaning.
    \item \textbf{Negative and null results define scope.} Near-ceiling synthetic performance does not imply real-data performance, graph propagation is not automatically beneficial on a small dense sensor graph, and non-significant clinical comparisons do not establish equivalence.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Next Tests}

The next study should begin with the raw, single-trial SHAPE data or an independent dataset; retain verified channel labels; and preregister one common preprocessing and evaluation pipeline. It should use nested subject-level evaluation, compare inductive performance with any transductive panel-normalization scenario, and report uncertainty at the subject level. Reservoir and graph ablations should change one component at a time, use shared folds, and avoid inference from overlapping fold scores.

Clinical evaluation should use comorbidity-aware or dimensional outcomes, prespecified multiplicity families, calibration and decision-curve analysis, external validation, and clinician assessment. Repeated sessions are needed before describing any quantity as trait-like or reliable. Channel-graph sensitivity should be checked across EEG references, frequency bands, phase-estimation methods, and graph thresholds.

\section{Closing Statement}

The defensible outcome is a technically explicit neuromorphic EEG analysis pipeline and a record of the values obtained in one cohort under specified transformations. The transformations can be inspected, corrected, and tested again. The post-defense audit narrows the original claims, corrects the supplied preprocessing and update code, and distinguishes archived descriptive results from the analyses required for prospective or clinical use.
