{\large \textbf{Abstract of the Dissertation}}\\[0.6cm]
{\large \textbf{Affective Reservoir-Spike Processing and Inference Network (ARSPI-Net):}}\\[0.2cm]
{\large \textbf{A Four-Level Interpretable Neuromorphic Framework}}\\[0.2cm]
{\large \textbf{for Clinical EEG Analysis}}\\[0.6cm]
{\large by}\\[0.2cm]
{\large Andrew Lane}\\[0.4cm]
{\large Doctor of Philosophy}\\[0.1cm]
{\large in}\\[0.1cm]
{\large Electrical and Computer Engineering}\\[0.1cm]
{\large Stony Brook University}\\[0.1cm]
{\large 2026}
\end{center}

\noindent
Affective EEG presents a central engineering challenge: emotionally relevant neural responses are distributed across time and electrodes, embedded in substantial participant-level variability, and often analyzed with models whose internal computations are difficult to examine. This dissertation develops the Affective Reservoir-Spike Processing and Inference Network (ARSPI-Net), a neuromorphic framework that transforms multichannel EEG into an explicit hierarchy of temporal, dynamical, and graph-organized representations. The architecture combines a fixed-weight Leaky Integrate-and-Fire reservoir with a six-bin spike-count code (BSC$_6$), electrode-resolved embeddings, dynamical response descriptors, and spatial-topological summaries. Membrane trajectories, spike rasters, temporal codes, graph measures, and cross-scale coupling remain available as named intermediate quantities throughout the analysis.

ARSPI-Net is evaluated using the Stress, Health, and the Psychophysiology of Emotion (SHAPE) Community dataset \cite{noauthor_shape_2018}. Quality control yields $211$ participants and $633$ observations for three-condition discrimination (Negative, Neutral, Pleasant), and $210$ participants and $840$ observations for four-category discrimination (Threat, Mutilation, Cute, Erotic). Under five repeats of five-fold participant-grouped cross-validation, the BSC$_6$/SRP-64 representation achieves $60.6\%$ balanced accuracy for three conditions and $53.2\%$ for four categories. These results exceed matched band-power/Hjorth representations by $11.3$ and $16.5$ percentage points, respectively. Participant-label permutation tests yield $p=0.0099$ for both tasks, and conventional-feature fusion increases three-condition performance to $62.0\%$.

Targeted destruction controls identify the structure responsible for this performance. Randomizing temporal-bin order reduces balanced accuracy by $7.4$ percentage points for three conditions and $12.9$ points for four categories; randomizing electrode identity reduces it by $13.9$ and $20.1$ points. Temporal collapse and population-count controls produce additional losses, demonstrating that the representation uses ordered temporal evolution and channel-specific organization rather than aggregate spike volume alone. The effect is stable across three fixed reservoir initializations, while temporal-window experiments show that discriminative information is distributed across the post-stimulus response. Raw ERP summaries provide a complementary reference at $68.1\%$ and $60.5\%$, clarifying the tradeoff between direct waveform information and the structured intermediate representation produced by the reservoir.

The dissertation's principal contribution is an experimentally characterized neuromorphic measurement chain connecting affective EEG to temporal spike codes, reservoir dynamics, and multichannel relational structure. Participant-grouped evaluation, mechanistic ablations, cross-granularity testing, and explicit intermediate representations provide a rigorous foundation for studying how temporal and spatial organization jointly support affective neural discrimination. ARSPI-Net thereby advances interpretable neuromorphic signal processing and establishes a focused platform for validation, architectural refinement, and hypothesis-driven clinical EEG research.
