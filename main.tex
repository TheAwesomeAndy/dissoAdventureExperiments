{\large Andrew Lane}\\[0.4cm]

\noindent
This dissertation introduces the Affective Reservoir-Spike Processing and Inference Network (ARSPI-Net), a staged neuromorphic framework for analyzing affective electroencephalography (EEG). It combines a fixed-weight Leaky Integrate-and-Fire reservoir, a six-bin spike-count code (BSC$_6$), and graph-organized spatial analysis. The design exposes membrane trajectories, spike counts, temporal bins, dynamical descriptors, graph metrics, and cross-scale coupling as inspectable quantities.

ARSPI-Net is evaluated on the Stress, Health, and the Psychophysiology of Emotion (SHAPE) Community dataset \cite{noauthor_shape_2018}, comprising $211$ participants, $34$ EEG channels, and three affective conditions. At the temporal level, the aggregate pre-reservoir bin profile corresponds with P300 ($r = 0.65$) and Late Positive Potential amplitude ($|r| = 0.82$); post-reservoir correspondence is weaker ($|r| = 0.23$ for LPP). At the geometric level, subject-associated variance exceeds condition-associated variance by a factor of $7.2$, and per-subject centering improves balanced accuracy by $13$--$21$ percentage points across seven tested representations. EEGNet achieves the highest centered accuracy ($89.1\%$), while the centered reservoir representation reaches $78.8\%$. At the dynamical level, seven named descriptors characterize reservoir responses, although their patient-level reliability is not established. At the systems level, temporal--spatial coupling exceeds an electrode-permutation null but is primarily observation-specific and does not improve classification beyond the better individual feature family.

Graph propagation does not outperform the non-propagated baseline across the tested operators, densities, depths, and adjacency constructions. Exploratory clinical analyses yield nominal associations for SUD, GAD, PTSD, and ADHD, but several are near chance, resolution-dependent, uncorrected for multiple testing, or not permutation-significant. In particular, the strongest SUD parametric comparison has $p = 0.0004$, whereas its label-permutation result is $p = 0.073$.

The principal contribution is therefore an explicit measurement chain rather than a claim of clinical deployment or best-in-class prediction. ARSPI-Net provides a reproducible framework for examining how temporal, dynamical, geometric, and graph-level representations change across a neuromorphic EEG pipeline, together with clear empirical boundaries that motivate independent validation.
